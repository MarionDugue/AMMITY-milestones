### Objectives:
The goal of this semester project was to develop at least one functioning prototype of a space cup. Three concepts in early development were improved and manufactured using silicone injection moulding: the flower, duckbill, and umbrella mechanisms originally developed by Simone Pfeiffer. In addition, a new concept - the balloon mechanism - was explored and initial development undertaken. The injection moulding process was investigated and optimized for these prototypes, including material selection, mould design, and manufacturing parameters. These efforts enabled the production of functional prototypes and provided insights into the feasibility, limitations, and future development potential of microgravity-compatible menstrual cup systems.
### Results & Learnings:
This project explored four different sealing mechanisms for a menstrual cup designed for use in microgravity: flower, duckbill, umbrella, and balloon. Silicone injection moulding proved effective for producing flexible prototypes, although each mechanism presented different manufacturing challenges.
The flower design emerged as the most mature solution. It provides sufficient containment volume, seals well, and is relatively easy to manufacture. Its main limitation is cleaning, which could be improved by redesigning removable components.
The duckbill and umbrella concepts show promise but require further refinement before microgravity testing. The balloon mechanism remains in an early exploratory stage, though progress was made in developing manufacturing techniques for thin silicone structures.
Overall, the project demonstrates that developing a menstrual cup suitable for microgravity is feasible. With further engineering, testing, and user-focused design improvements, such a device could offer astronauts a reusable alternative to hormonal menstrual suppression during long-duration missions.
### Next Steps:
The next steps of the project focus on refining the most promising designs and further evaluating their practical use. The flower mechanism will be tested in microgravity through parabolic flight experiments to better understand its performance and identify areas for improvement. At the same time, the cleaning procedure will be optimized by redesigning the cups to reduce small parts and enclosed spaces that are difficult to clean. Additional studies will investigate the long-term durability of the cups under repeated folding and cleaning cycles. Manufacturing processes will also be improved by refining mould designs to enable faster and more reliable production. Finally, user experience and medical considerations will be explored by collecting feedback on insertion, removal, and cleaning, as well as consulting gynaecological experts to assess potential health risks.
